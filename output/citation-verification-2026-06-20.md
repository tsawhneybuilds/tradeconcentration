## Summary

All five claim-citation pairs were verified against the extracted full text of the locally stored papers. No citation mismatch was found.

- Panagariya and Bagaria (2013) use Lorenz curves and Gini coefficients to document concentration of trade values across strictly positive product lines and trading partners.
- Their Gini is bounded between zero and one, with larger values indicating greater concentration.
- Cadot, Carrere, and Strauss-Kahn (2011) calculate Herfindahl, Theil, and Gini indices, report similar fitted concentration shapes, and use Theil as the analytical focus.
- Cadot et al. choose Theil because its additive decomposition maps the between component to the extensive margin and the within component to the intensive margin.
- Hummels and Klenow (2005) independently establish that extensive and intensive trade margins have distinct economic content; they estimate that the extensive margin accounts for about 60 percent of larger economies' greater exports.
