## Audit 5: Econometrics

### Identification Assessment

The main estimand is descriptive. The pooled/year-FE specifications identify a cross-country development-stage curve within year, conditional on log population and oil export share. They do not prove that countries mechanically reconcentrate as they grow richer.

### Major Concerns

1. The strongest product hump appears in level PPP pooled/year-FE models. It weakens under country fixed effects and mostly disappears under log PPP.
2. The original log-GNI/world-relative Cadot tribunal result does not show a meaningful hump: the quadratic p-value is 0.735 and the turning point is far outside support.
3. The mechanism evidence is mixed: continuing-product superstar scaling flags 59.7% of reconcentration episodes; old-cone pruning flags 14.8%, even though the old-cone mismatch interaction is statistically significant.

### Minor Concerns

1. The level-PPP turning points around $65k-$79k are substantially higher than the classic Cadot benchmark, partly because the price base, period, sample, and outcome construction differ.
2. Multiple outcomes and functional forms are being searched. The narrative should distinguish pre-specified Cadot-comparable checks from exploratory diagnostics.

## Verdict

Major revision for any paper claim that "Cadot is confirmed." Minor revision for an internal empirical memo that says "level PPP restores a pooled Cadot-style product-concentration curve, but log and country-FE diagnostics show the result is fragile."

## Prioritized Recommendations

1. Lead with level PPP as Cadot-comparable, but immediately show log PPP and country-FE sensitivity.
2. State clearly that pooled/year-FE evidence is a cross-country development-stage pattern.
3. Do not call the mechanism primarily "old-cone exit"; say the statistically cleanest mechanism result is old-cone-consistent exit, while the episode mass is mostly continuing-product scaling.
4. Add influence diagnostics: leave-one-country-out, high-income-tail exclusion, oil-exporter exclusion, and Section 16/HS aggregation sensitivity around the PPP hump.
