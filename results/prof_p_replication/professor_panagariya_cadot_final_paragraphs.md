# Paragraphs for Professor Panagariya

Professor Panagariya, our combined results suggest that Cadot's diversification hump is best understood as a cross-country product-composition pattern rather than a universal path followed by individual countries. In the modern 2000–2024 panel, pooled level-PPP regressions show the expected decline and later rise in export product concentration, and the between-country Gini and Theil curves have turning points near $73,000 in constant-2021 PPP dollars. However, country fixed effects eliminate the export Gini and Theil humps. The visual contrast is shown in the website's [PPP results](https://tsawhneybuilds.github.io/trade-gini-map-old/cadot-hump.html#cadot-ppp-results) and [pooled-versus-country-FE section](https://tsawhneybuilds.github.io/trade-gini-map-old/cadot-hump.html#cadot-pooled-country-fe).

The between-country pattern appears to reflect persistent differences in country type rather than a common income-driven transition. Between-country differences account for about 73% of modern Export Product Gini variation, 94% of Theil variation, and 81% of HHI variation. The rich-country tail combines broad industrial exporters with oil exporters, entrepôts, microstates, and financial or tax hubs. The curve survives leave-one-country-out and simple oil/microstate exclusions, so it is not a single-outlier artifact, but its turning point is composition-sensitive. The [world-product exposure analysis](https://tsawhneybuilds.github.io/trade-gini-map/exposure/#exposure-gdp-alignment) also shows that larger economies align more strongly with products that are large in world trade, even after broad primary commodities are removed.

The mechanism evidence is narrower than Cadot's strongest interpretation. We do detect an old-cone-consistent relationship between income-product mismatch and product exit on the rich side, but it is not the dominant pattern. Continuing-product or “superstar” scaling appears in about 60% of modern reconcentration episodes, compared with roughly 21% flagged for old-cone pruning. The relevant website exhibits are the [mechanism scorecard](https://tsawhneybuilds.github.io/trade-gini-map-old/cadot-hump.html#cadot-mechanisms) and the [old-cone results](https://tsawhneybuilds.github.io/trade-gini-map-old/cadot-hump.html#cadot-old-cone).

The long-run evidence further limits the scope of the claim. In the 1827–2014 historical partner panel, none of six preferred tests and none of 168 sensitivity tests supports diversification followed by partner reconcentration. This does not disprove Cadot's product result, but it shows that the hump is not a general law of trade concentration. My final interpretation is therefore that Cadot captures a real equilibrium relationship across countries in product space, while our evidence does not support a universal within-country development law or a predominantly extensive-margin mechanism. A literal verdict on the original paper still requires the matched 1988–2006 mirror-export/product-universe reconstruction or a genuinely comparable historical product bridge.
